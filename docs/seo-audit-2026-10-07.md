# JurisPage SEO, Analytics, and Conversion Audit

**Audit date:** October 7, 2026  
**Primary objective:** Generate qualified leads that become JurisPage clients, while identifying firms that are a better fit for Juris Digital.  
**Data reviewed:** Google Search Console, GA4, the live production site, the current sitemap, production logs, and the local Next.js codebase.

## Executive summary

JurisPage has a sound technical base and meaningful organic visibility, but it is not yet converting that visibility into measurable demand. The clearest opportunity is not publishing more pages immediately. It is to repair measurement, recover lost authority from legacy URLs, improve the handful of pages already close to page one, and make the offer and qualification path consistent from landing page through CRM and closed client.

The central findings are:

1. **Measurement is the most urgent problem.** GA4 recorded no key events and no form-submission events during the last 90 days. The current setup cannot reliably answer which source, page, or campaign creates qualified leads or clients.
2. **Organic visibility is broad but shallow.** Search Console recorded 175,000 impressions but only 142 clicks over three months. Most non-branded terms rank outside the first page, and branded searches account for a large share of clicks.
3. **There are several high-value near-term SEO wins.** The law-firm SEO cost and Scorpion alternative pages already rank near page one. Content-writing, Google Ads, and certain practice-area topics also have substantial impressions within striking distance.
4. **Legacy 404s are wasting traffic and link equity.** GA4 recorded 192 views of the 404 page in 90 days, Search Console reports 14 known 404 URLs, and production logs show requests to many legitimate former content and service URLs.
5. **The site is technically cleaner than the performance numbers imply.** All 180 sitemap URLs returned 200, used self-referencing canonical URLs, and had a title, description, H1, and structured data. No sitemap URL redirected, and no duplicate titles or descriptions were found.
6. **The offer and qualification path need tightening.** The site sometimes says services are available individually, although JurisPage is sold as a package. The contact form also cannot distinguish a $5,000/month prospect from a $20,000+/month Juris Digital prospect.
7. **The site has too many low-performing template pages relative to its authority.** Search Console reports 58 crawled-but-not-indexed and 77 discovered-but-not-indexed pages. More location/practice combinations should not be added until existing pages are evaluated, differentiated, consolidated, or removed.

## Business and audience framework

The acquisition system should identify and route three distinct segments:

| Segment | Likely need | Commercial path |
|---|---|---|
| New or smaller law firm with little established presence | A complete, dependable marketing foundation | Standard JurisPage package, generally $2,500–$4,000/month |
| Established firm seeking focused growth | Custom SEO, paid media, conversion, or market expansion work delivered as a cohesive engagement | Custom JurisPage engagement, generally $4,000–$20,000/month |
| Larger or more sophisticated firm with complex growth needs | Multi-channel strategy and higher-touch engagement | Qualified handoff to Juris Digital at approximately $20,000+/month |

Every major landing page and form should help identify firm maturity, practice area, market, growth objective, budget band, and urgency without making the experience feel like a long sales questionnaire.

## Priority findings and recommendations

### P0 — Repair conversion measurement and protect data quality

This should be completed before judging page or campaign performance.

1. **Create a single, controlled GA4 implementation.** The global layout currently loads Google Tag Manager and a direct GA4 `gtag` configuration. Audit the GTM container and consolidate ownership so pageviews and events cannot be duplicated or inconsistently configured.
2. **Map custom `dataLayer` events to GA4.** The code pushes Market Gap events, but those events do not appear in GA4. Configure GTM triggers and GA4 event tags, then verify them in Tag Assistant and GA4 DebugView.
3. **Remove personally identifiable information from analytics.** The Market Gap form currently sends email and firm name in the `market_gap_step_1_complete` data-layer event. Email must not be sent to GA4. Use non-PII properties and a generated lead identifier instead.
4. **Track successful outcomes, not just form starts.** Add `market_gap_submitted` and `contact_submitted` only after the server confirms success. Mark both as GA4 key events.
5. **Persist attribution.** Current forms read UTM parameters only from the active page URL. Store first-touch and latest-touch source, medium, campaign, landing page, and referring domain so attribution survives navigation before form submission.
6. **Connect CRM stages to acquisition.** Pass a stable anonymous lead ID into HubSpot and the application database. Use it to report qualified lead, sales-accepted lead, opportunity, and client revenue by source and landing page.
7. **Clean traffic quality.** Define internal traffic, review referral exclusions and bot patterns, and investigate unexpectedly large Direct and overseas traffic segments.

Recommended event model:

| Event | When it fires | Key event? | Useful non-PII properties |
|---|---|---:|---|
| `primary_cta_clicked` | User clicks a main CTA | No | CTA label, page, placement, destination |
| `market_gap_started` | First meaningful interaction | No | landing page, source, content cluster |
| `market_gap_step_completed` | Each completed step | No | step number, practice area, market, firm stage |
| `market_gap_submitted` | Server confirms submission | Yes | lead ID, budget band, firm stage, form source |
| `contact_submitted` | Server confirms contact submission | Yes | lead ID, budget band, practice area, form source |
| `phone_clicked` | Phone CTA clicked | Yes | page, placement, source |
| `meeting_booked` | Scheduling platform confirms booking | Yes | lead ID, meeting type |
| `report_delivered` | Market Gap report is sent | Offline lifecycle event | lead ID, turnaround time |
| `report_viewed` | Prospect views report | Offline/web lifecycle event | lead ID, report section engagement |
| `mql_created` / `sal_created` / `sql_created` | CRM qualification milestones | Offline lifecycle events | lead ID, qualification reason |
| `client_won` | Deal closes | Revenue outcome | lead ID, product tier, revenue band |

### P1 — Make the offer and funnel consistent

1. The homepage sends “See My Market Gap” visitors to `/growth-report/`, while navigation uses `/see-my-market-gap/`. Choose one canonical funnel entry and redirect the other so reporting, testing, and links are unified.
2. Remove or rewrite the homepage claim that services are available individually. It conflicts with the intended bundled-package model and may attract low-fit buyers.
3. State who JurisPage is best for above the fold or immediately after the hero. The current “Get More Cases” promise is clear but broad.
4. Add tier-aware qualification. The current highest budget choice is `$5,000+/month`, which cannot identify a $20,000+/month Juris Digital fit. Use bands that support the actual routing model without making the form feel exclusionary.
5. Reduce uncertainty around the Market Gap deliverable: what the instant result includes, what the human-prepared report includes, when it arrives, and what happens afterward.
6. Shorten or sequence the contact form. Seven required fields create unnecessary friction before trust is fully established. Ask only what is needed for routing, then progressively collect detail.
7. Place the most relevant case study and outcome proof beside each commercial CTA, not only on a separate case-studies page.

### P1 — Fix sitemap and crawl signaling

- Keep only the canonical `https://jurispage.com/sitemap.xml` submission in Search Console. Remove obsolete `www` and old WordPress sitemap submissions after confirming they are no longer needed.
- The sitemap currently assigns a fresh build-time `lastModified` date to nearly every static page. Use actual content modification dates or omit `lastmod`; otherwise every deployment falsely signals that all pages changed.
- Investigate the one indexed URL reported as blocked by robots.txt.
- Review the 58 crawled-currently-not-indexed and 77 discovered-currently-not-indexed URLs by template, demand, uniqueness, internal links, and backlinks.

### P2 — Consolidate and improve the programmatic page inventory

The site should not keep expanding location/practice combinations while many existing pages remain unindexed or rank beyond page three.

For each template family:

1. Keep pages with demonstrated impressions, meaningful local/practice demand, unique proof, or a realistic business-development use.
2. Merge overlapping pages when intent is indistinguishable.
3. Noindex or remove pages that cannot offer unique value and have no links or demand.
4. Add unique market evidence, firm examples, FAQs, regulatory/local context, testimonials, and relevant case studies to retained pages.
5. Strengthen hubs so search engines and users can understand practice-area and location relationships.

Use a decision table with URL, template, impressions, clicks, position, indexed state, organic landing sessions, backlinks, assisted leads, unique content score, and recommended action.

### P2 — Build authority around original evidence

The most competitive head terms will require more than on-page revisions. Recommended authority assets include:

- Annual law-firm SEO and paid-media benchmarks
- Legal marketing cost and conversion benchmarks by practice area
- A transparent Market Gap methodology
- Interactive budget or opportunity calculators
- Data-backed studies of local-pack, AI-search, or intake performance
- Detailed case studies organized by firm stage, practice area, market, and channel
- Expert author pages and clear review/update standards for SEO and AI content

Digital PR and relevant legal-industry partnerships should promote these assets. Redirect recovery should occur first so existing authority is not wasted.

### Metadata observations

No duplicate titles or descriptions were found. Seventeen titles exceeded 60 characters, including pricing, best SEO companies, SEO cost, the Market Gap page, core SEO/local SEO, content writing, and several case-study/blog pages. Rewrite based on actual pixel width and SERP intent rather than applying a rigid character limit. The practice-areas page title is only 26 characters and is underdeveloped.

## Technical health

### Working well

- 180/180 sitemap URLs returned HTTP 200.
- No sitemap URL redirected.
- Canonical URLs were self-referencing and consistent.
- HTTP redirects to HTTPS; `www` redirects to the non-`www` canonical host.
- HSTS is enabled.
- Robots.txt allows public crawling and identifies the correct sitemap.
- No missing title, meta description, H1, canonical, JSON-LD, or server-rendered image alt attribute was detected on sitemap URLs.
- No duplicate title, description, or H1 was detected in the sitemap set.
- Production logs showed no HTTP 500 responses in the preceding 24 hours.
- The local production build completed successfully during project setup.

### Risks and limitations

- Search Console has insufficient field data to report Core Web Vitals for mobile or desktop.
- A PageSpeed Insights lab test could not be completed because the API returned a quota-limit response. Performance therefore remains unverified, not “passed.”
- The homepage HTML response is approximately 155 KB, static assets are material in size, and multiple third-party scripts load globally. A focused performance pass should measure JavaScript execution, LCP, INP, and form responsiveness on mobile.
- HubSpot, GTM/GA4, Clarity, Turnstile, and other global integrations should be loaded and consented intentionally, with each tool justified by a measurement requirement.
- Form delivery was reviewed in code and production error logs, but no fake production lead was submitted. A controlled end-to-end test should verify database storage, email delivery, Slack notification, HubSpot creation, attribution fields, and GA4 events.

## Recommended 90-day roadmap

### Days 1–14: establish trustworthy measurement and stop leakage

1. Document the funnel stages and definitions for lead, MQL, SAL, SQL, opportunity, and client.
2. Consolidate GA4/GTM ownership and implement the event model.
3. Remove PII from analytics events.
4. Persist first-touch and last-touch attribution into forms and HubSpot.
5. Create GA4 key events and a source-to-client dashboard.
6. Build and deploy the first high-confidence legacy redirect map.
7. Unify the Market Gap URL and correct the “services available individually” message.
8. Run a controlled production submission test through every destination.

### Days 15–45: capture existing demand

1. Refresh `/law-firm-seo-cost/` and `/scorpion-legal-marketing-alternative/`.
2. Improve content-writing and Google Ads pages using query-level Search Console data.
3. Strengthen About, Pricing, and case-study proof paths.
4. Add tier-aware qualification and shorten the initial contact experience.
5. Correct sitemap `lastmod`, remove obsolete sitemap submissions, and resolve the robots anomaly.
6. Complete the indexation decision table for every programmatic template page.

### Days 46–90: build compounding authority

1. Consolidate the law-firm SEO topic cluster and improve internal-link architecture.
2. Publish one original benchmark or research asset supported by digital PR.
3. Create case-study hubs by practice area and firm stage.
4. Improve or prune low-value metro/practice combinations.
5. Launch controlled CTA, form, and proof experiments only after conversion tracking is verified.
6. Review lead quality and closed-client contribution by source, not merely traffic or form volume.

- Market Gap starts, completions, and completion rate
- Contact starts, completions, and completion rate
- Phone clicks and meetings booked

- Mobile LCP, INP, and CLS once sufficient field data is available

## Final assessment

JurisPage does not have a basic crawlability or metadata crisis. It has an acquisition-system problem: visibility is not yet concentrated on page-one commercial rankings, conversion outcomes are not measured, legacy authority is leaking through 404s, and the website-to-CRM path does not cleanly identify which firms fit JurisPage versus Juris Digital.

The fastest path to revenue is:

1. make every lead and qualification stage measurable;
2. recover legacy traffic and link equity;
3. improve the small set of high-intent pages already near page one;
4. simplify and align the offer and funnel;
5. consolidate low-value pages and invest in original evidence that earns authority.

That sequence will create trustworthy feedback before additional content production or broad redesign work.
