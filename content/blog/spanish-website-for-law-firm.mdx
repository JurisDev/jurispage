Firms ask us this constantly, usually framed as a someday project. Here is the practical framework we give them, because for most firms the answer is yes, and sooner than they think.

## The three questions that decide it

**Does your market speak Spanish?** Not hypothetically. Look at your metro's demographics, your intake logs, and the calls your front desk already fields in Spanish. The US has more than 42 million people who speak Spanish at home, and they are concentrated in the exact metros where most case volume lives. If your city is one of them, demand already exists; it is simply routing to the few competitors who acknowledge it.

**Can your intake handle it?** A Spanish website answered by an English only phone line converts badly and reflects worse. You need at least one reliable path for a Spanish speaking caller: a bilingual staffer, an answering service with Spanish coverage, or intake routing that flags Spanish leads for the right person. Solve this before launch, not after.

**Will you treat it as a channel or a checkbox?** A checkbox is a translated homepage nobody measures. A channel is a full Spanish site with its own URLs, its own rankings, and its own line in your reporting: views, leads and calls from Spanish pages. Channels get investment because they show returns. Checkboxes get quietly abandoned.

## What a real Spanish site requires

The technical bar is well documented by Google: Spanish pages at their own URLs on your domain, served as real HTML rather than a JavaScript widget, with correct hreflang tags, translated titles and descriptions, and sitemap coverage. The editorial bar is higher and more often missed: Spanish that matches how prospects search (abogado de accidentes, not a literal rendering of car crash attorney), and disclaimers translated faithfully, because bar advertising rules apply in every language you publish.

The ownership bar matters most over time. Some translation services keep your translations on their servers and take everything with them when you cancel. Whatever you choose, confirm your translations live in your own website where nobody can repossess them.
