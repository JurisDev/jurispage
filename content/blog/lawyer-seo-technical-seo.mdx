Your content can be excellent and your firm still invisible. When Google cannot crawl, render, or index an attorney website, the rest of your [lawyer SEO](/law-firm-seo/) work never gets a chance to pay off. This page stays on one thing: the technical layer. Crawlability, site speed, structured data, and indexation, and the specific ways each one breaks on law firm sites.

This is not a keyword guide or a content guide. It is the plumbing.

## Crawlability: can Google reach every page

Crawlability decides whether search engines ever see a page in the first place. On attorney websites the failures cluster in a few predictable spots.

A redesign ships and the new `robots.txt` still carries `Disallow: /` from the staging server. The entire site goes dark. You watch organic traffic fall off a cliff two weeks later and assume Google penalized you. It did not. You told it to leave.

Practice area pages render client side. The browser shows a full page on personal injury claims, but the raw HTML Google fetches first is close to empty, and the firm wonders why a detailed page reads as thin. Location pages get generated by the dozen with one city swapped in, then nothing links to them, so they sit as orphans no crawler walks to.

Check the rendered HTML, not the pretty browser view. Google Search Console's URL Inspection tool runs "Test Live URL" and shows you what Googlebot actually receives. If your practice area copy is missing from that rendered output, your content is not the issue. Your rendering is.

## Indexation: crawled is not the same as ranking

A page can be crawled and still never make it into the index. Search Console's Pages report is where you confirm this. You compare the number of pages you expect against the number marked indexed, then read the exclusion reasons.

"Crawled, currently not indexed" on a stack of location pages usually means Google looked, decided the pages were near duplicates of each other, and declined to store them. Forty city pages with the city name find-replaced into the same template is the classic version. The fix is not more pages. It is fewer, genuinely distinct pages: local court detail, real case history for that venue, an office that exists.

A `noindex` tag survives a launch and quietly keeps your services section out of search for months. A canonical tag points every paginated page back at page one and buries the content beyond it.

There is also a size reality worth knowing. Googlebot is efficient about how much raw HTML it processes per page. Practice area pages stuffed with inline scripts, inline styles, and thousands of unnecessary DOM nodes push the content that matters (your contact form, your internal links, your case results) further down the file than the crawler reliably reads. Move scripts and styles into external files. Keep the DOM lean.

## Site performance: the metrics that punish law firm pages

Google measures three Core Web Vitals, and attorney websites tend to fail the same ones for the same reasons.

LCP, how fast the main content paints, dies on a 4MB hero photo of the courthouse that nobody compressed. CLS, layout shift, comes from images with no width and height reserving space, so the page jumps while a potential client is reading. INP, how fast the page responds to every interaction, is the one that catches law firms off guard, because firms love interactive tools. A settlement calculator or a multi step intake form that takes longer than 200ms to respond to a click drags the whole score into the red.

The weight usually is not your content. It is the marketing stack bolted on top. A live chat widget loading several hundred kilobytes before the page renders. Call tracking dropped into the head as a blocking script. Three session recording tools running at once because nobody removed the old one.

## Structured data: tell search engines what your firm is

Structured data is how you state, in machine readable terms, that this is a law firm, these are its attorneys, this is the office, these are the services. Done right it makes your firm legible to both search engines and the AI answers pulling from them.

Use `LegalService` for the firm rather than a generic business type. Put `Attorney` schema on each bio page and connect it with `sameAs` to the state bar profile and other official listings, so the credential is verifiable rather than asserted. Use `FAQPage` only for questions that actually appear on the page, because marking up answers a visitor cannot see is the kind of thing Google strips.

The error we find most: schema that disagrees with the page. The address in the markup does not match the address in the footer, or the phone number was updated on the site and never in the JSON-LD. Mismatched signals are worse than missing ones.
